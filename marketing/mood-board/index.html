A paper list at the shop down the road, not a supermarket app.

The feeling

What it is

Warm, handwritten, unhurried. The customer writes a list the way they always have; the shop prices it the way it always has. The app only carries the paper between them — so the paper is the hero, and the software stays out of the way.

What it is not

Not ten-minute delivery. No countdown timers, no flashing offers, no urgency. Nothing here should feel like it is rushing the customer or the shopkeeper — the whole promise is that you deal with a person you already know.

Photographs are taken in the shop, on white chart paper, in daylight. Not stock, not scraped — partly because that is the law, and partly because a customer seeing the actual packet on the actual shelf is the one thing a supermarket app can never show them.

Rules worth keeping

Each of these was learnt the hard way on a real phone, not chosen from taste.

Show the product wholecontain, never cover. Cropping beheads a tall bottle and trims a wide packet — the parts that identify it.
White behind a product, warm everywhere elsea pack shot on white sits seamlessly in a white tile; the rest of the app stays on sand.
Leave room for Hindithe same sentence runs longer in Devanagari. Any column sized to fit the English will break the Hindi.
Ask the phone, don't guessbottom bars use the safe-area inset. A hard-coded 32dp sat under Android's 48dp navigation bar.
No touch target that reaches past its rowa 10dp hitSlop on a 44dp row overlapped its neighbours and swallowed scrolling for days before it was found.
No letter-spacing on Devanagaritracking detaches a matra from its letter — "पहले" renders as "प ह ले". It is an English habit; keep it for Latin wordmarks only.
No urgency theatreno timers, no "only 2 left!", no blinking. The shop is not racing anyone.
No pure black, no pure greyevery neutral carries warmth. Pure #000 or #888 looks like a different product.
No borrowed imagerynot from Amazon, not from a stock site, not with the watermark removed.
